If someone reaches out

Someone who trusts you may send you a message during a hard moment. Here’s a short, plain guide to being there for them.

This is general guidance, not professional or medical advice.

What a “rough moment” text usually means

They’re riding out an urge and want some company for a hard few minutes. They usually aren’t asking you to fix anything. Hearing from you is often enough.

What tends to help

  • Reply, or call back, if you can. A short message counts.
  • Listen more than you talk.
  • Stay calm and kind. Your steadiness helps.
  • Ask what would help right now.
  • Offer to walk or talk with them for a few minutes.
  • Check in later to see how they’re doing.

What to avoid

  • Lectures or long advice.
  • Blame or shaming.
  • Making it about you.

It’s okay to set limits

You don’t have to be available all the time. It’s fine to say, “I can’t talk right now, but I’m free at 7.” Being honest about when you can be there is still a kind answer.

When to get more help

If they talk about hurting themselves, or you think they’re in danger, get help right away.

Call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US, 24/7). In an emergency, call 911. For substance use or mental health treatment referrals, the SAMHSA National Helpline is 1-800-662-4357 (free, confidential, 24/7). Urge Walk is not a crisis service.

Thank you for being someone they trust.
